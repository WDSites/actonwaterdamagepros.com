Emergency Roof Leak Water Damage in Acton, MA

A roof leak soaks insulation, ceilings and walls long before the stain shows. We dry and clean the inside, and a roofer fixes the roof. Call the 24/7 line.

Roof leak water damage is what happens inside the house after the roof lets water in. The roofer fixes shingles and flashing. We deal with the wet insulation, soaked ceiling, damp framing and any mold risk that follows. Both jobs matter, because a patched roof over a wet attic still leaves damage in the house.

Wind drives much of it in Acton. The town's 2018 hazard mitigation plan says nor'easters strike once or twice a year with gusts up to 70 miles per hour, and that four nor'easters hit in March 2015 alone. Wind lifts shingles and snow loads stress flashing, so a roof can fail at a valley, chimney or vent without looking damaged from the street.

What A Roof Leak Looks Like Inside

Water usually travels before it shows. It follows a rafter, runs along a ceiling joist and drops at a light fixture or a seam far from the real entry. That is why the position of a stain is a clue but not proof.

First Hour Safety Steps

When water is coming through a ceiling

Stay out from under a bulging ceiling. Wet drywall is heavy and can drop without warning.

If water is near a fixture or fan, turn off that circuit at the panel only if the panel is dry. Otherwise call the electric utility or 911.

Put a bucket under the drip and move furniture and electronics out of the area. Do not poke a bulging ceiling yourself.

Do not climb onto a wet or icy roof. Photograph the damage from the ground and the interior instead.

Ice Dams And Snow Melt In Acton

Snowfall of 48 to 72 inches a year, as the hazard plan reports, produces ice dams when heat escapes into the attic and melts snow that refreezes at the eave. Water backs up under shingles and enters at the edge of the wall. Massachusetts residential code section R905.1.2 calls for an ice barrier in cold regions, extending from the eave up the roof, and the Acton Building Department can say what applies to your roof.

Ice dams cause wet insulation, stained walls near the eaves and ceilings that look fine until the thaw. The fix on the roof is the roofer's. The fix inside is ours.

How We Dry A House After A Roof Leak

We begin with moisture mapping of the ceiling, the walls below it and the attic floor if we can reach it. Wet insulation is bagged and removed because it holds water against the framing. Soaked drywall that has lost its strength comes out in clean cuts, and dry material around it stays.

Then we set air movers to push air across the wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers to remove the moisture they release. A thermal imaging camera helps us see cold, wet areas behind finished walls. We read meters on each visit and document the drying against the targets in ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference.

If the ceiling is replaced, water damage repair handles the drywall, texture and paint. For standing water in a finished space, emergency water removal covers the first extraction.

What Changes The Cost

How long the roof leaked is the biggest factor. A short leak into one ceiling bay is small. A slow leak that soaked insulation across a room and ran down an exterior wall is larger. Other factors are ceiling height, attic access, texture type and whether the wall below needs openings.

Roofing repair is a separate trade and a separate bill. We explain our scope and price before work starts, and we separate interior drying from anything a roofer or builder will do.

Insurance And Roof Leaks

State insurance regulators at the Massachusetts Division of Insurance separate sudden interior water damage from gradual problems. A storm that opens the roof and lets water in is closer to sudden. A leak that has run for months through worn flashing is gradual. Take photos of the roof and the interior, and write down dates.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Painting a stain before the area is dry and the roof is fixed.
  • Leaving wet attic insulation in place for weeks.
  • Climbing up in wet or icy conditions for a closer look.
  • Assuming the stain sits right under the hole in the roof.
