Emergency Storm Water Damage in Acton, MA

Storms send water in through roofs, windows, foundations and basements. Call the 24/7 line once it is safe, and we handle extraction, drying and cleanup.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Storm water damage is the water that gets into a home during or after severe weather. It can come from above through a damaged roof, from the side through windows and siding, or from below as groundwater rises. The common thread is that it arrives fast, often to many homes at once, and it carries dirt.

Acton's 2018 hazard mitigation plan, a town planning document, says nor'easters strike one or two times a year with gusts up to 70 miles per hour, and records 72 thunderstorm events in Middlesex County from 2006 to 2017. It also notes an 87 mile per hour wind on July 7, 2014. Wind-driven rain finds gaps that ordinary rain never reaches.

How Storm Water Gets Into Acton Homes

The plan lists Hurricane Irene in 2011 and Hurricane Sandy in 2012 among the storms that affected the area, and notes a tropical storm that tracked through the southeast corner of Acton in 1996. It also reports that roads flood about every other year and that the Butter Brook culvert at Route 27 still floods periodically.

Fallen limbs and wires are a real concern as well. The plan gives a 100-year wind speed of 110 miles per hour and notes that falling trees and power lines are an issue in town, so storm damage can come with a roof opening you did not expect.

First Hour Safety Steps After A Storm

Before you go back in after a storm

Stay away from downed power lines and anything touching them. Call 911 and the utility.

Do not enter a basement with standing water if the panel or outlets are submerged.

Watch for sagging ceilings and loose roofing. Do not climb onto a wet roof.

Treat floodwater as contaminated. Avoid skin contact and keep children and pets out.

Storm water inside? Call the 24/7 line once it is safe to talk and move.

How We Clean Up After Storm Water

We begin with extraction using submersible pumps or extraction units, then remove soaked insulation, carpet pad and drywall that cannot dry. Surfaces are cleaned and treated. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and meters track the progress.

When storm water carried soil or outdoor debris, we treat it as contaminated unless a technician finds otherwise. ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference, describes how water categories guide the work. The approach for outdoor water is more conservative than for a clean pipe leak.

For a flooded basement with rising groundwater, see basement water damage cleanup. For a roof that failed, see roof leak water damage.

What Changes The Cost

The volume of water, how many rooms it reached, how contaminated it was, how long it sat and how many materials need to be removed all matter. After large storms, equipment and crews can be in demand, and we are honest about timing. Scope and price are covered before work begins, even on a busy day.

Insurance And Storm Damage

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that flood and sewer backup are generally excluded from standard homeowners policies unless added. Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof is a different cause from rising groundwater. Document the cause as clearly as you can and ask your insurer which coverage applies.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Walking into a flooded basement before power is off.
  • Ignoring a small roof opening because the rain stopped.
  • Waiting days to start drying.
  • Throwing away evidence before you photograph it.
