Boxborough's Wet Seasons

Spring thaw and heavy September storms are the two times Boxborough homes get wet. The town's 2024 hazard mitigation plan lists a 2023 emergency declaration for severe weather and flooding that included Storm Lee. It also says flooding affects many roadways, including Depot Road and Route 111, and that several culverts have been replaced to address it.

Beavers add another twist. Members of the planning committee cited a constantly blocked man-made dam due to beavers, and the plan notes a beaver dam on MBTA property next to a privately owned dam on Guggins Brook. When a beaver dam fails or backs up, water arrives fast and in places that were dry the week before.

Winter brings outages. The plan notes that backup power exists only at the Fire Station and Police Station, so a long outage can stop sump pumps in homes. If water is rising and the pump is silent, call the line.

Close the valve

If a line failed, shut the main. A house on a private well may also need the pump switch turned off.

Power and water do not mix

Stay out of water near outlets. If the sump pump is off and the basement is filling, call your electric utility or 911 about power before wading in.

Raise and record

Lift valuables and photograph everything before moving it.

Private Wells, Septic And Water Damage

Boxborough has no municipal water supply beyond a small system serving town buildings. The plan counts 24 privately owned public water supply systems. Littleton Water Department serves Central Street and parts of Leonard Road, and everyone else uses private wells. The town also has no municipal sewer system.

That changes how we handle floods. If floodwater reaches a wellhead or septic area, the water inside the house may be contaminated beyond what a basement flood normally carries. We treat storm or septic backup as Category 3, and we recommend testing your well after flooding by asking your local health department how.

What To Do Next: Insurance, Permits And Records

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says flood damage is generally not covered by a standard homeowners policy. Water from a backed-up drain is also generally excluded. Check your policy, photograph the damage and wait for the insurer's inspection before permanent repairs. Permit rules vary, so ask the Boxborough building department.
