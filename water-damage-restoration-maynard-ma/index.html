Mill-Era Housing And Water Damage

Maynard grew around the Assabet Woolen Mill, and the town was incorporated in 1871. Its Presidential district was built in 1902 and 1903 as worker housing, starting with 206 houses. The town's 2024 hazard mitigation plan reports that 28 percent of Maynard's homes were built before 1940, compared with nearly 32 percent statewide.

Century-old houses mean plaster ceilings, old pipe runs and basements built before modern waterproofing. A leak in one of these homes can travel a long way inside a wall before it shows. We map moisture with meters and thermal imaging so the repair plan reflects where water went, not just where it showed up.

When Water Is Spreading In Maynard

If water is spreading, call the 24/7 line. Close the main valve if a pipe failed, lift valuables off wet floors and keep out of water near outlets or appliances. Maynard's Water and Sewer Division says after-hours emergencies go through the Police Department, so if the water is a public main problem, report it there.

The Assabet River And Maynard's Low Spots

The Assabet River runs through the center of Maynard. The Assabet gage in Maynard reached 7 feet in March 2010 against a flood stage of 5 feet, according to Acton's hazard mitigation plan. Maynard's own plan lists flood-prone stretches of Routes 27, 62 and 117 and notes that Town Hall, next to the river, flooded in its basement about a decade ago.

The town's report flags the Assabet River Walk, the Rockland Avenue soccer fields and homes north of Summer Street as vulnerable, so those are places to watch after heavy rain. If your basement has a floor drain, check it before a storm.

Sewer, Water Quality And Insurance In Maynard

More than 95 percent of Maynard's residents are on the public sewer system. The town's resilience report describes a collection system made mostly of vitrified clay and asbestos cement pipes from the early to mid twentieth century, and says overwhelmed pipes are a storm concern. If sewage backs up into your house, it is Category 3. Stay out, do not use water and call the line.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance notes that water from backed-up drains or sewers is generally not covered by a standard homeowners policy unless you added an endorsement. Check your policy. Permit rules vary, so ask the Maynard building department whether your rebuild needs a permit.
