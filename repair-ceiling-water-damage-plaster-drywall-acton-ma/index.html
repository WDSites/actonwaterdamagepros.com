Plaster vs Drywall Ceiling Water Damage in Acton, MA Homes

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Plaster ceilings and drywall ceilings fail differently after a leak, and the repair is different too. Drywall sags and softens. Plaster cracks, bulges and drops pieces, because the plaster is held up by wood lath, not by a paper face. Knowing which you have decides the repair method, the cost and the safety precautions.

Older Acton homes often have plaster, and newer ones have drywall. If you are not sure, a magnet and a small hole are enough to tell. This guide compares the two and shows what to do in each case. For drywall specifics, see our guide to [drywall water damage.

How To Tell Which You Have

CluePlasterDrywall
Age of the homeOften built before the 1950sCommon after the 1950s
ThicknessThicker, about three quarters inch or more with lathHalf inch or five eighths inch sheets
Back of a cut pieceRough plaster keys through wood lath slatsPaper face and gypsum core
Nail testNail goes into lath strips, hard to hit betweenScrew or nail goes through board into joist
CracksSpider cracks, often curvedStraight seams along tape lines

A magnet and a small test hole give a quick answer. Check wiring first.

What Water Does To Each

Drywall absorbs water quickly, sags between joists and turns soft. Plaster resists water longer, but when it gets wet it can lose its grip on the lath. The keys, which are the blobs of plaster pushed through the gaps in the lath, can break. A plaster ceiling can look fine and then drop a section, which is dangerous overhead.

A bulging plaster ceiling is a warning sign. Do not stand under it, and keep people and pets out. If a section is loose, a professional can support or remove it safely. Plaster is heavy, and a fall can injure someone.

Repair Options For Plaster

Common plaster ceiling repairs

  • Re-securing loose plaster with plaster washers or adhesive and screws.
  • Skim coating over small cracks and stains after drying.
  • Patching holes with plaster or setting-type joint compound.
  • Replacing a damaged section with drywall cut to match the thickness.
  • Removing and replacing the whole ceiling if it is widely damaged.

Many plaster ceilings are repaired in place because replacing them is major work. Re-securing loose plaster and skim coating is a skilled trade, so ask about the contractor's experience with plaster, not just drywall. A mismatched patch can leave visible ridges under raking light.

Lead Paint And Older Homes

Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule requires contractors who disturb paint in such homes for pay to be certified and to follow lead-safe practices. If you plan to sand, scrape or cut into old paint yourself, read EPA guidance first and keep children and pregnant women out of the work area.

Dust control is the main point, so wet cleanup, plastic sheeting and HEPA vacuums matter. Ask any company that works on an older ceiling whether it follows lead-safe practices. We cannot promise that every old ceiling contains lead or does not, so assume the possibility until tested.

Plaster In Acton's Older Housing

Acton's 2018 hazard mitigation plan reports that about 10 percent of housing units were built before 1940. Houses around Acton Center are among the older stock, and roof and plaster problems tend to appear first in them.

Older homes also tend to have older roofs and flashing. Acton's average annual snowfall of 48 to 72 inches means roof loads and ice dams are a recurring winter issue, and a slow roof leak can soak a plaster ceiling for weeks before it shows.

Questions For A Plaster Contractor

What to ask before you hire

  • How many plaster ceiling repairs have you done in homes of this age?
  • Will you re-secure loose plaster, patch or replace?
  • How will you handle dust and old paint, including lead-safe practices?
  • How will the repair blend with the rest of the ceiling?
  • What happens if the lath or framing above is damaged?
  • What is included in the price and what is not?

Plaster work is a specialized trade and good plasterers are not always easy to find. A drywall contractor may suggest replacing the ceiling with drywall, which is a legitimate choice but changes the look and may cost more. Ask each contractor to explain why they recommend what they do.

Temporary Protection Until The Repair

If a repair has to wait, protect the room. Move furniture out from under the damaged area, cover floors with plastic and keep people out of the room if plaster is loose. Do not hang anything heavy from a wet ceiling. Take photos each day so you can see whether the damage is growing.

Why Plaster Is Worth Saving When It Can Be

Original plaster is thick, quiet and fire resistant, and many owners of older homes prefer to keep it. A skilled plasterer can often save a ceiling that looks beyond repair. Replacing it with drywall is faster, but it changes the look of cornices, medallions and edges and may disturb old paint.

The right choice depends on how much of the ceiling is loose, how wet the lath is and what the owner wants. Ask for both options in writing, with scope, so the decision rests on facts and not on a sales pitch.

Whichever route you choose, dry the ceiling first. A repair over damp lath fails within a season and can trap moisture where mold grows unseen.

Drying A Plaster Ceiling

Plaster dries more slowly than drywall, and the lath and framing above it need to dry too. Crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find wet areas, and may open a small inspection hole above the plaster. Drying protects against mold, which EPA says can be controlled by controlling moisture.

After drying, repair begins. Water damage repair describes how the rebuild stage is scoped. If you live in an older home in Concord or Maynard, the same plaster considerations apply.
