When Water Is Spreading In Concord

Concord has an unusual water map. The Sudbury and Assabet Rivers meet in town to form the Concord River, and the town's 2023 hazard mitigation plan lists about 40 locally identified flood areas. That is a lot for a town of roughly 18,500 people, and it explains why we get calls in more than one neighborhood.

The plan names Concord Center, which floods about once every ten years because Mill Brook runs under historic buildings. It lists Cambridge Turnpike, which floods yearly over about two miles, and the Fitchburg Turnpike, where roads close three to four times a year. It also names the Main Street underpass by the MBTA, which flash floods, and Westvale Meadows Condominiums.

If you live near any of those, a storm warning is a reason to move valuables out of the lowest level before the rain, and to know where your main valve is. If water has already entered, call the line and stay out of it until power is confirmed safe.

Stop the source

If a pipe let go, close the main valve. If the water came from outside, there is nothing to shut, so move to the next step.

Mind the power

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry to reach. Otherwise call your electric utility or 911 before calling us.

Lift and photograph

Raise furniture and electronics off wet floors and photograph the damage for your insurer before moving things.

Keep clear

Keep children and pets out of any water that could be contaminated, and call the 24/7 line.

Flood Events That Shaped Concord Claims

The plan lists regional floods in 1978, 1979, 1987, 1991, 1996, 1998, 2001, 2004, 2006, 2007, 2010, 2013 and 2018. March 2010 was a federal disaster, with 17.7 inches of rain recorded at Blue Hill Observatory over three storms. Middlesex County flood events from 2010 to 2022 totaled $42.06 million in property damage, and $35.2 million of that came from March 2010.

The plan counts five repetitive loss structures in Concord, two residential and three commercial, with 18 losses between them. That small number tells us something useful. Serious repeat flooding is concentrated in specific spots, so most Concord homes face leaks and appliance failures more than rivers. We handle both.

Older Concord Houses And What Water Does To Them

Concord is known for its historic houses, and many residential streets have older construction with plaster walls, original floor framing and stone or brick foundations. Water travels through those materials differently than through new drywall, and some old plaster can hold moisture long after the surface feels dry. The assessment uses meters, not touch.

The same plan reports 7,066 housing units and 26 percent renter occupancy. If you rent in Concord, tell your landlord about water damage as soon as you see it and keep your photos. A renter's policy and the landlord's policy cover different things.

Water Utility, Permits And Insurance In Concord

Concord's Water and Sewer Division runs the town system, which has six active groundwater wells and one surface supply and about 121 miles of main. It also has emergency interconnections with Acton and Bedford. If the problem is the main or a service line before your meter, call the division. If the break is inside your house, call us.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Concord building department whether your rebuild needs a permit. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says flood damage and water from backed-up drains are generally not covered by standard homeowners policies. Check your policy and call your insurer before permanent repairs.
