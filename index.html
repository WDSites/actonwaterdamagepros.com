Water damage restoration is the work of removing water, drying the building to measured targets and repairing what the moisture damaged. The published industry standard for the first two parts is ANSI/IICRC S500, and it is fair to ask any company you call whether its crews work to it. Mold work follows a separate standard, S520.

Why Acton Homes Get Wet

Local Water Problems We See On Calls

Acton drains into the Assabet River through two stream systems, Nashoba Brook and Fort Pond Brook. The town's 2018 hazard mitigation plan rates flooding as a high-frequency hazard and notes that roadways flood about every other year, though they usually stay passable. For a homeowner that means a wet spring can raise groundwater around a foundation even when the street looks fine.

The same plan points to beaver activity on nearly every brook and says freezing temperatures followed by rain can block catch basins. Both push water toward low spots near houses. If your yard slopes toward the foundation or a catch basin on your street stays clogged, a finished basement is the first place the water will show up.

Big storms matter too. The plan records 14.83 inches of rain in March 2010, the wettest month on record, with the Assabet River gage in Maynard reaching 7 feet against a flood stage of 5 feet. More recently, a 2023 storm that the town's trails page estimated at 4.1 inches of rain washed out a culvert at Camp Acton and flooded the Assabet River trail and canoe launch. If a storm like that has just passed, check basements and crawl spaces before you assume the house stayed dry.

Housing age adds another layer. The same plan reports that about 10 percent of Acton's housing units were built before 1940, and older ceilings and walls may be lath and plaster instead of drywall. That changes how water travels and how repairs are done, which is why the assessment comes before any cutting.

Acton's villages each have their own mix. Homes near the brooks in West Acton and South Acton deal more with groundwater, newer developments such as Nagog Woods in North Acton lean toward supply-line and appliance leaks, and older houses around Acton Center tend to show roof and plaster problems first. If you are near the Acton Arboretum or NARA Park, low ground and ponds are close enough that a heavy week of rain is worth watching.

Know The Water

What The Water Category Means For Your Home

Restoration crews sort water into three categories because the category decides what can be dried in place and what must be thrown out. This is the decision framework we walk through at the assessment.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
Category 1Broken supply line, sink overflow, rain through a roofExtraction and drying in place. Act quickly because it does not stay clean for long.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without solidsExtraction, removal of wet porous materials such as carpet pad, cleaning and drying.
Category 3Sewage backup, river or groundwater floodingContaminated materials are removed, surfaces are cleaned and disinfected, and the area is dried.

Categories follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 framework. A technician confirms the category on site, and water can move up a category the longer it sits.

Time is the quiet variable. Clean water that sits on carpet or in a wall cavity for a day or two can start to behave like Category 2, and EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to keep mold from growing. That window is the reason the line is staffed overnight.

Before You Call Anyone

What To Do While You Wait For A Crew

Safe steps you can take right now

  • Stop the source if you can reach it safely, starting with the main water valve.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If in doubt, stay out and call your electric utility or 911.
  • Lift furniture, rugs and electronics off wet floors and move valuables to a dry room.
  • Keep children and pets out of any water that may be contaminated, such as sewage or flood water.
  • Photograph or video everything before moving it, because your insurer will ask.

If the water came from the Acton Water District's main or your service line, the District says an employee is on call 24 hours a day for emergencies, and its number is on its website. For anything inside the house after the meter, the restoration line is the right call.

Insurance Claims

Working With Your Adjuster

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that flood damage is generally not covered under a standard homeowners policy, and that water from a backed-up drain or sewer is also generally excluded. Sudden and accidental leaks are more often covered, and gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Your policy language decides, so read it and call your insurer or agent promptly.

The Division also advises photographing the damage, making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects, keeping every receipt and avoiding permanent repairs before the adjuster has looked. We follow the same order of work: document, protect, dry, and only then rebuild.

Documentation That Supports Your Claim

We photograph the damage, record moisture readings and write down the scope of work so your adjuster has clear records to review. Coverage decisions belong to your insurer, so read your policy and call your agent early.

Choosing A Company

Questions Worth Asking Any Restoration Company

Whoever you call, ask the same few questions. Do the crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500 for water and S520 for mold? Will you explain the scope and price before work starts? Will you record moisture readings so drying can be documented? Are the people on site licensed and insured? Clear answers to those four are worth more than any slogan.

Restoration is not waterproofing. We dry and rebuild what water damaged, but if groundwater keeps entering through a foundation wall, the long-term fix belongs to a waterproofing or drainage contractor. We will say so plainly rather than let you pay for drying that will be undone by the next storm.
