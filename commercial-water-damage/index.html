Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Acton, MA

A wet office, shop or small building loses time with every hour of water. Call the line and we will explain what we can do and how the work would run.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

Commercial water damage means water in a space where people work or store things. The physical process is the same as in a house. Water comes in, spreads and soaks. What changes is how much floor area is involved, how fast a space needs to be usable again and how many people need access to it.

This page explains what we do, and it says plainly what we do not do. We extract water, map and dry the structure, remove soaked materials, clean and treat surfaces and tell you what we see. We do not manage building systems, fire damage or hazardous materials.

Where Commercial Water Damage Starts

The 2018 hazard mitigation plan, a town planning document, notes that commercial development in Acton is concentrated at the Nagog Office Park in North Acton and that Route 2A businesses serve surrounding communities. Space in those areas ranges from single offices to buildings with multiple suites sharing walls, ceilings and mechanical rooms.

Typical sources are a failed supply line above a ceiling, a leaking roof, a backed-up drain, a sprinkler pipe that froze and a storm that sent water under a door. Because suites share structure, water from one can travel into the next.

First Hour Safety Steps

When water gets into a commercial space

Close the water supply or zone valve if you know where it is.

Shut off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry. Otherwise call the electric utility or 911.

Move people out of the wet area and keep them away from sagging ceiling tiles and wet floors.

Photograph the source and damage, and lift stock, files and electronics off the floor.

How We Dry A Commercial Space

We extract standing water with pumps and extraction units and then map moisture with meters and a thermal camera. Wet ceiling tiles, insulation and soaked drywall are removed. Carpet and pad are lifted if they cannot be dried in place. Dehumidifiers and air movers are sized to the volume of the space.

Larger spaces mean larger equipment, more power draw and more planning about circuits and access. We discuss where equipment goes and how long it will run, and we document readings each visit against ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference for water damage restoration.

Contents are handled carefully. We can move and protect items in the work area, but we do not appraise or restore specialized equipment. After drying, water damage repair covers the rebuild. For standing water, see emergency water removal.

What Changes The Cost

The factors are floor area, ceiling height, the amount of water, the finishes and how many days of drying are needed. Larger spaces take more equipment, and finished suites with ceilings and carpet take more removal than a warehouse floor. Scope, access and price are talked through before work begins.

Insurance And Commercial Policies

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that sudden water damage may be treated differently from gradual damage. Commercial policies and leases add other terms, and the owner, the tenant and their insurers may each have responsibilities. We keep photos, readings and a written scope so the records are ready for whoever needs them.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Waiting for the property manager and doing nothing.
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles in place.
  • Running the HVAC system through wet areas before they are dry.
  • Letting stock sit on wet floors.
