Basement Water Damage Cleanup in Acton, MA

Basements are where Acton water problems start. Call the line and we pump, remove wet materials, dry the structure and explain what comes next.

Basement water damage is the most common kind we see in Acton. The ground around a house gets wet, a sump pump fails, a window well fills, a pipe lets go, and the lowest floor is where it all ends up. Concrete does not absorb much, but everything stored or built on it does.

A basement flood has two jobs. The first is getting the water out safely. The second is making sure that the walls, framing and stored items do not stay wet long enough to grow mold. Both depend on speed and on knowing where the water came from.

Why Acton Basements Get Wet

The 2018 hazard mitigation plan, a town planning document, reports that March 2010 brought 14.83 inches of rain and that the Assabet River gage reached 7 feet against a flood stage of 5 feet. It also says roads flood about every other year and that freezing followed by rain can block catch basins, which pushes water toward foundations.

The plan names flood-prone areas including Stow Street and Martin Street, which flood about every three to four years, as well as Great Road near Wetherbee, Flint Road and Nashoba Brook between Route 2A and Concord Road. FEMA flood records in the plan list five repetitive-loss single-family structures with 13 losses from 1987 to 2010. Even outside those areas, a wet spring can overwhelm a marginal sump pump.

First Hour Safety Steps

Before you go into a wet basement

Do not enter if water is near the electrical panel, outlets or the furnace. Call your utility or 911.

If you must go down, turn off power at the main breaker only if you can reach it dry. Otherwise stay out.

Treat floodwater from outdoors or a drain as contaminated. Avoid skin contact.

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

Water in the basement? Stay out until power is off and call the 24/7 line.

How We Clean Up A Basement

We pump out standing water with submersible pumps and finish with extraction units. Wet carpet pad, insulation, stored boxes and drywall that cannot dry are removed, with a clean cut above the high-water line. Concrete is cleaned and treated with antimicrobial.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the space, and desiccant equipment can help in cold, damp conditions. Meters check framing, sill plates and wall bases until they reach drying goals. ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference for water damage restoration, sets out the water categories and the approach.

If the water stayed too long and mold is present, see mold remediation. For a larger flood affecting the whole house, see flood damage restoration. For carpet, see carpet water damage.

What Changes The Cost

How deep the water was, how long it sat, how much is finished space and whether it was clean or contaminated all matter. A bare basement is quick. A finished one with walls, carpet and furniture takes longer. Scope and price are explained before the first pump goes in.

Insurance And Basement Water

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that flood and sewer backup are generally excluded from standard homeowners policies unless added, and that sudden interior water damage may be treated differently from a gradual problem. A broken pipe is a different claim from groundwater. Photograph the water level and the sump.

Prevention And Mistakes To Avoid

  • Test the sump pump each spring and consider a battery backup.
  • Keep gutters clean and downspouts pointed away from the house.
  • Do not store cardboard boxes directly on the floor.
  • Do not leave wet carpet or insulation in place.
