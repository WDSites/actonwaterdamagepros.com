How to Remove Mold From a Basement in Acton, MA by Surface

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

How to remove mold from a basement depends on what the mold is growing on. Hard, non-porous surfaces such as sealed concrete and metal can usually be cleaned. Porous materials such as drywall, carpet, insulation and cardboard usually have to be removed and replaced. That surface-by-surface rule is the practical core of EPA's mold guidance, and it is more useful than any single cleaning recipe.

This page assumes the patch is small enough to handle yourself, under about 10 square feet per EPA. Larger or hidden mold is a job for a professional, and mold remediation explains how that works. Fix the moisture source first, whatever the surface.

Concrete And Block Walls

Poured concrete and block walls hold dark stains, but not all stains are mold. White crusty deposits on basement block are often efflorescence, which is mineral salt left by moving water, and they point to moisture even though they are not mold. Brush them dry and watch where they return, because the pattern shows where water is entering.

For real mold on concrete, scrub with detergent and water, rinse and dry the surface completely. EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine part of cleanup, and dead mold still needs to be removed, not just killed. Dry the wall with a dehumidifier before you paint, because sealing a damp wall hides the problem.

Framing, Plywood And Wooden Shelves

Solid wood can often be cleaned if mold is on the surface and the wood is still sound. Scrub, dry fully and look for soft spots. A screwdriver that sinks easily into a joist or sill plate means rot, and structural wood is a job for a contractor, not a cleaning task. Plywood and particleboard shelves are cheaper to replace than to clean.

Drywall, Insulation And Carpet

Porous materials absorb mold deeper than the surface. If drywall or paneling has visible mold across a patch, plan to cut it out and replace it. Insulation that was wet and moldy goes out. Carpet and carpet pad that grew mold usually go too. Bag the material in heavy plastic before carrying it through the house to limit spreading spores.

Stored Items And What To Do With Them

Boxes, books, fabrics and furniture stored in a damp basement are the most common mold casualties. Hard items like plastic bins, metal and glass can be wiped and dried. Fabric, upholstered pieces and paper are often easier to replace than to clean. If something is valuable or sentimental, ask a restoration company about cleaning options before you throw it away.

A safe order of work for a small cleanup

  • Fix the leak, seep or condensation that fed the mold.
  • Remove and bag moldy porous materials and stored items.
  • Clean hard surfaces with detergent and water and dry them completely.
  • Run a dehumidifier and a fan to bring humidity below 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50.
  • Recheck in a week and again after the next heavy rain.

What Acton's Flood Maps Mean For A Basement

Acton's hazard mitigation plan lists FEMA Zone A and Zone AE areas along Fort Pond Brook, Nashoba Brook, the Boxborough boundary and the northern tip of town. If your basement is in or near one of those mapped areas, the town's GIS viewer and the FEMA flood map for your address will show it.

Even outside a mapped zone, basements can take on water. The plan identifies five repetitive loss structures in town, all single-family homes, with 13 losses between 1987 and 2010 totaling $60,396. Repeated wetting is what turns a one-time cleanup into a recurring mold problem, so fixing drainage matters more than the surface you scrub.

None of this equipment is exotic, and most of it can be bought at a hardware store in one trip. The one item worth borrowing or renting if you do not own it is a HEPA vacuum. After scrubbing, fine dust settles on every surface, and a HEPA vacuum is the safest way to pick it up.

Wash work clothes separately in hot water, and shower after finishing. If you notice symptoms during the cleanup, stop and talk to your physician. We do not make health claims about mold.

When To Stop And Call

Stop if you find mold across a large area, if the wall behind the drywall is wet, if the material crumbles when you press it or if the water was sewage. Also stop if anyone in the house has health concerns, because EPA advises talking to a health professional before cleanup. A crew can contain the area and remove material without spreading it.
