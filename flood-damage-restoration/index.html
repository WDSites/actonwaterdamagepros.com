Emergency Flood Damage Restoration in Acton, MA

Flood damage restoration in Acton covers storm water, rising groundwater, basement flooding and sewage backup. The emergency line is open 24/7, and the first minutes are about safety.

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

If water is entering your home right now, treat it as an emergency and call the 24/7 line. Flood water is usually Category 3, which means it may carry sewage, soil and chemicals. While you wait, keep people and pets out of the water, shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe, and do not touch electrical equipment that is wet or standing in water.

Safety Before You Do Anything Else

Electrical and sewage safety comes first

If water touches outlets, the panel or appliances, stay out. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing completely dry. Otherwise call your electric utility or 911 before calling us.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911. Raw sewage is a health hazard, so keep everyone away from it until a crew arrives.

When Flooding Is An Emergency In Acton

Acton's 2018 hazard mitigation plan lists regional flood events in August 1954, March 1968, January 1979, April 1987, October 1996, June 1998, March 2001, April 2004, May 2006, April 2007 and March 2010. March 2010 was the worst, with 14.83 inches of rain recorded and the Assabet River gage in Maynard reaching 7 feet against a flood stage of 5 feet. The plan rates flooding in Acton as a high-frequency hazard.

For a homeowner the lesson is that flooding is not rare here, and the places it happens are specific. The plan names flood-prone locations including Stow Street and Martin Street, which flood roughly every three to four years, Great Road near Wetherbee Street, Flint Road, and Nashoba Brook between Route 2A and Concord Road. If you live near those corridors, a heavy storm deserves a basement check.

The town also reports a 2023 storm, estimated by its trails page at 4.1 inches of rain, that washed out a culvert at Camp Acton and flooded the Assabet River trail and canoe launch. Damage showed up throughout town, not only on conservation land.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

Most Acton flood calls are basements. Water arrives through a floor drain, a wall crack, a window well or a failed sump pump. The first job is extraction with submersible pumps and wet vacuums, followed by removal of anything porous that has soaked up the water, such as carpet, pad, insulation and the bottom of drywall.

Then the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers, and surfaces are cleaned. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours, which is why speed matters even when the water looks clean. A finished basement with wall-to-wall carpet is the case where waiting costs the most.

Sewage Backup Cleanup: What To Do First

A sewage backup is a safety problem before it is a cleanup problem. Stay out of the affected area, keep children and pets away, and do not run water or flush toilets because that can push more into the house. EPA guidance says that if water was sewage or other contaminated water, you should use a professional with experience in that kind of damage.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance notes that water from backed-up drains or sewers is generally not covered under a standard homeowners policy, though some insurers sell an endorsement. Check your policy for sewer backup wording before you assume either way.

Water coming in or a sewer backing up? Call the 24/7 emergency line now, before you start cleaning.

Flood Insurance And FEMA Maps

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and needs a separate flood policy. The town says FEMA maps decide which properties must carry flood insurance, and Acton's GIS viewer shows flood zones as an approximate representation of those maps. To find your own zone, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurance agent about coverage.

Acton's plan identifies FEMA Zone A and Zone AE areas along Fort Pond Brook, Nashoba Brook, the Boxborough boundary and the northern tip of town. A home outside the mapped floodplain can still flood, so a clear map does not mean zero risk.

Crawl Spaces And Whole-House Water

Some calls involve water across a whole level, for example after a pipe failure while the family was away. These jobs need more extraction, more removal and more drying equipment, and they are where moisture mapping matters most. We use thermal imaging and meters to find water hiding in walls and under floors before the drying plan is set.

If the problem began as a leak and has been going for days, mold may already be growing. See mold remediation for how containment and removal work. If the structure is wet but stable, the approach is described under water mitigation.
