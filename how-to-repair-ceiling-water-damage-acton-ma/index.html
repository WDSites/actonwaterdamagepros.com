How to Repair Ceiling Water Damage in an Acton, MA Home

☎ Call (978) 252-8637

To repair ceiling water damage, stop the leak, let the ceiling dry fully, cut out any drywall that is sagging, stained through or soft, patch with new drywall, then prime and paint. Skipping the first two steps is the usual reason a stain comes back. A ceiling that looks dry on the surface can still hold water in the insulation above it.

This is a how-to for a typical drywall ceiling. Plaster ceilings, which are common in older Acton houses, are covered in a separate guide on [plaster versus drywall. If water is still coming through, call before you start, because the source decides everything else.

Step One: Stop The Water And Protect The Room

Find where the water is coming from. A roof leak shows after rain or snowmelt. A plumbing leak shows when a fixture runs, often from a bathroom, kitchen or laundry above. An air-conditioning drain line leaks when the system is cooling. Shut off the water to a leaking fixture if you can, and place buckets and plastic sheeting under the drip.

If the ceiling is bulging, water is trapped above it. Place a bucket under the lowest point and puncture the bulge with a screwdriver to let the water drain in a controlled way rather than letting it collapse. Wear eye protection and stay out of the path of the water. If the ceiling has a light fixture or outlet near the leak, keep the power off to that circuit.

Before you cut anything

  • Turn off the circuit to any light fixture that is wet.
  • Move furniture, cover floors and keep children and pets out.
  • Take photos for your insurer before you touch anything.
  • Check the attic or the room above for the source.
  • Wear gloves, eye protection and a dust mask for cutting.

Step Two: Dry Everything

Drywall that stays wet will grow mold and weaken. Open windows, run fans and a dehumidifier and, if there is attic insulation above that got wet, pull it back so it can dry. EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. A moisture meter, which costs $20 to $60 at a hardware store, tells you whether the ceiling and framing have actually dried.

Step Four: Cut, Patch And Finish

Mark a square or rectangle around the damaged area and cut to the center of the nearest joists so the new patch has something to screw into. Remove the damaged piece, check the framing and insulation above for dampness and fill the opening with a new piece of drywall cut to size. Screw it to the framing, tape the seams and apply joint compound in thin coats, sanding between coats.

Apply a stain-blocking primer before the finish coat, or the old stain can bleed through the paint. Match the ceiling sheen and finish the whole ceiling plane if the repair is large, because patched spots show under angled light. Texture ceilings need a matching spray or hand-applied texture, which is trickier than it looks.

Matching Texture And Finish

Texture is the part that gives away a patch. Flat ceilings are the easiest to match. Orange peel, knockdown and popcorn finishes each need a particular technique, and a poorly matched patch shows under angled light. Test your technique on a scrap piece of drywall before you touch the ceiling.

If the texture is difficult, repainting the entire ceiling plane in one go hides small differences. Work in daylight from a window if you can, since light from the side shows flaws that overhead lighting hides. Allow each coat to dry fully before the next, because rushing is the most common cause of a patch that cracks later.

When To Call A Professional

Call if the area is large, if the ceiling has partially collapsed, if the water was not clean or if mold is visible. Also call if you are unsure the leak has stopped. A crew can extract water, dry the cavity above the ceiling and cut out wet material safely, and a rebuild contractor or water damage repair team can finish the patch and paint.
