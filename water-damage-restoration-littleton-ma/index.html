Water damage restoration in Littleton, MA starts with where you live. Lake neighborhoods, Littleton Common and North Littleton each have different water habits. Our crews work from Nagog Park in Acton, and the emergency line is open 24/7. Same-day response is offered when available.

Long Lake, Spectacle Pond And The Lake Neighborhoods

Littleton's 2018 resilience summary describes Long Lake as a 99-acre glacial lake with about 600 houses, mostly on septic systems and roughly one-third-acre lots. Lake Wattawanakee and Spectacle Pond are also named. Lakeside houses sit close to the water table, so a wet season can show up as a damp basement even without a pipe failing.

The summary notes that flooding of houses, mostly basements, occurs routinely in areas with a high water table or poor natural drainage from ledge or tight soils. It also says residential septic systems are a concern if the water table rises. If you live near a lake, that combination is worth watching.

When Water Is Spreading In Littleton

If water is spreading, call the 24/7 line. Shut off the main valve if a pipe failed, lift valuables off wet floors and keep out of water near outlets. Littleton Electric Light and Water Departments serve the town, so an outage or main problem goes to them while an interior leak goes to us.

Valve and pump

Close the main valve for a pipe failure. For a basement filling with groundwater, check whether the sump pump has power.

Keep dry hands on the panel

Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing dry. Otherwise call your electric utility or 911.

Photos first

Photograph rooms, ceilings and floors before you move anything.

Call the line

We are open all night. Tell us if your basement is below a lake or pond level.

Littleton Common, North Littleton And The Roads In Between

Littleton Common is the town center, and North Littleton sits toward Westford. Routes 2, 2A, 110 and 119 and Interstate 495 cross town. The resilience summary says Littleton has had little to no flooding of roadways, so most damage here starts in basements and plumbing, not rivers.

It does flag one spot, Taylor Street, where beaver dams could cause flooding. And it describes an older mobile home park on Ayer Road as a vulnerability. If your property is near a beaver-affected wetland, treat a long wet spell as a reason to check the basement.

Climate, Insurance And Permits In Littleton

State resilience work for the SuAsCo basin projects winter precipitation to increase 1 to 23 percent by mid-century. Projections are not predictions for any one year, but they point the same direction as a high water table. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says flood damage is generally not covered by a standard homeowners policy, so ask your agent about separate coverage.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Littleton building department whether your rebuild needs a permit. Photograph damage, keep receipts and wait for your insurer's inspection before permanent repairs.
